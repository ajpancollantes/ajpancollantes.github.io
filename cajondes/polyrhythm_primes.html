Mathematical Foundations: Chebyshev's $\psi(x)$

1. The Second Chebyshev Function

The Chebyshev function $\psi(x)$ is defined as the sum of the von Mangoldt function $\Lambda(n)$:

\psi(x) = \sum_{n \le x} \Lambda(n) = \sum_{p^k \le x} \ln(p)

It increases by a step of height $\ln(p)$ whenever $x$ reaches a prime power $x = p, p^2, p^3, \dots$.

2. Logarithmic Transformation $t = \ln(x)$

In original variable $x$, prime power events get exponentially spread out. By taking $t = \ln(x)$ (or $x = e^t$), prime powers occur at $t = k \cdot \ln(p)$.

\frac{d}{dt} \psi(e^t) = \sum_{p} \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \ln(p) \cdot \delta\left(t - k\ln(p)\right)

Taking the distributional derivative yields a sum of Dirac delta impulses. Notice that for each fixed prime $p$, the impulses occur at equal intervals $\Delta t = \ln(p)$, forming a perfect periodic percussion train infinitely into $t \to \infty$!
